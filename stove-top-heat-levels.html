How burner size affects cooking

Under construction

Burner size directly impacts how heat spreads across your pan, which heavily dictates whether your food cooks evenly or burns. Because home stovetops vary wildly in power (measured in BTUs for gas), a recipe's instruction for "medium heat" is never a universal dial setting. Instead, recipe instructions assume you are using an appropriately sized burner for the pan required, typically calibrated to a standard medium-sized burner.

Matching Burner Size to Pan

Burners primarily heat the area directly above them, relying on the pan's metal to pull that heat outward to the edges.

Burner too small for the pan: This creates a severe hot spot in the center and cold zones at the edges. If you try to sear a large steak in a large pan over a tiny burner, the center will burn before the edges even brown.

Burner too large for the pan: Heat escapes up the sides of the cookware. This wastes energy, can scorch or melt your pan's handles, and burns food pooling at the pan's edges while leaving the center undercooked.

The Ideal Match: Your burner should roughly match the diameter of your pan's base (not the top rim). As a rule of thumb, gas flames should cover about 75% of the pan's bottom, and electric/induction elements should cover 60–70%.
