Roux

Under construction

A roux, from the French word for "red," is a mixture of roughly equal volumes of a starch and a liquid fat that are cooked together and then used as a thickener for liquids in soups, stews, and sauces. It's a common technique in Continental cuisine (French, Italian, et cetera), and is also used heavily in the Cajun and Creole cooking of Louisiana for thickening dishes like gumbo and étouffée.

Ingredients

  • 1 part flour
  • 1 part butter or other fat

Instructions

  1. Over medium-high heat, melt salted butter in a saucepan.
  2. Gradually whisk in ¼ cup all-purpose flour using a wooden spoon or whisk.
  3. Stir constantly for at least 5 minutes until the mixture is smooth and lightly golden in color.
  4. Remove the saucepan from heat and let the roux cool slightly before using it to thicken your dish.
  5. Incorporate the roux into soups, stews, or sauces as needed, stirring constantly to avoid lumps.
